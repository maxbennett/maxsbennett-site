The Book

A Brief History of Intelligence: Evolution, AI, and the Five Breakthroughs That Made Our Brains

Mariner Books, 2023 · Translated into 10+ languages · Cited in 50+ peer-reviewed papers

Equal parts Sapiens, Behave, and Superintelligence, but wholly original in scope, A Brief History of Intelligence offers a paradigm shift for how we understand neuroscience and AI. It chronicles the five “breakthroughs” in the 600-million-year evolution of human intelligence, and reveals what the brains of the past can tell us about the AI of tomorrow.

About

I'm a PhD candidate in computer science at Columbia University, advised by Rich Zemel. My research sits at the intersection of neuroscience and machine learning: I build neural memory systems for language models that can be instructed, in plain language, what to learn and what to remember. My earlier work in evolutionary neuroscience asked the reverse question — what the 600-million-year history of brains can teach us about building intelligent machines.

Alongside research, I've spent a decade building AI companies. I cofounded Bluecore, where I serve as Chief Product Officer and whose machine-learning products are used by some of the largest retailers in the world, and Alby AI, an enterprise AI-agent platform acquired by Bluecore in 2024. I serve on the board of the Thousand Brains Project and was named to Forbes 30 Under 30.

Away from the lab, I compose jazz, perform long-form improv, tinker with small video games in Unity, and read too much philosophy of mind.
